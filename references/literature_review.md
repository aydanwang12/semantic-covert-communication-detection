# Literature and novelty audit

Verified against primary sources on 2026-11-08. This is a focused review of the six requested works, not a systematic review. Publication versions matter: Anwar et al. v3 adds closely overlapping experiments; NARCBench v5 changes its author list and results substantially relative to v1.

## Core references

1. **Denamganai (2026), PoPLi.** *Detecting Covert Collusion between AI Agents through Partner-Outsider Positive Listening*, arXiv:2610.37849v1, submitted October 27. The paper compares partner and outsider action distributions under meaning-preserving paraphrases, with unchanged-message replay noise correction. Its experiments use scripted first-letter messages in a simulated market. Proposition 5 and the limitations explicitly identify meaning-carried codes as a blind spot. Our response-gap design builds directly on this work; a single action-change estimate is a simplified proxy, not a reproduction of its full distributional estimator. [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37849); [method and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37849v1).

2. **Zhou and May (2026), Conceptual Steganography.** arXiv:2605.26537v1, submitted May 26. Encodes bits in high-level reasoning behaviors, compares with a matched keyword baseline, and tests ordinary and strategy-aware paraphrasers across four model families and two domains. Consequently, semantic/conceptual encoding and its greater paraphrase robustness are established prior work, not this project's contribution. [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26537); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26537v1).

3. **Anwar et al. (2026), A Decision-Theoretic Formalisation of Steganography With Applications to LLM Monitoring.** arXiv:2602.23163v3, first submitted February 26, revised April 29. Defines a receiver-sentinel usable-information gap. Crucially, Appendix A already compares six structural, syntactic, and semantic channels under two paraphrasers, with 1,000 summaries per channel; Tables 7-8 report receiver-sentinel accuracy gaps. Semantic variants include underwriting frames and opposite interpretations of facts. This substantially overlaps any generic claim to compare semantic versus syntactic detection. Their accuracy/utility gap differs from our intervention-induced response-change gap. [Primary metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23163); [v3 Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23163v3#A1).

4. **Motwani et al. (2024), Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography.** Published in *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37*, main conference track, DOI 10.52202/079017-2336. Formalizes secret collusion, evaluates model capabilities, and studies limitations of monitoring, paraphrasing, and related mitigations. It establishes the broader threat model; this project is not a first demonstration of multi-agent covert communication. Cite the verified 2024 proceedings publication rather than confusing a later arXiv update with initial publication. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/861f7dad098aec1c3560fb7add468d41-Abstract-Conference.html); [paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/861f7dad098aec1c3560fb7add468d41-Paper-Conference.pdf).

5. **Rose et al. (2026), Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent Interpretability.** arXiv:2604.01151v5, first submitted April 1, revised October 15. Introduces NARCBench and, in this version, HELIOS group-level detection statistics using activation probes. Includes a steganographic blackjack holdout. This is complementary white-box evidence, not the same black-box replay design. The v5 author list is Aaron Rose, Carissa Cullen, Sahar Abdelnabi, Philip Torr, Brandon Gary Kaplowitz, and Christian Schroeder de Witt. Avoid importing v1 results into a v5 citation. [Primary metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01151); [v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01151v5); [author code](https://github.com/aaronrose227/narcbench).

6. **Krzyzanowski et al. (2026), Hand and Brain: Defenses against Agentic Steganography in Language Models.** Primary OpenReview PDF identifies Robert Krzyzanowski, Ivan Arcuschin, Matthew Lee, Bryce Meyer, and Georg Lange. It studies payloads carried by strategy choices and a trusted-prefix defense, including mathematical, perspective, code, and writing settings. It independently establishes that preserving high-level choices can preserve a covert channel. The original PDF was verified through the search index; direct OpenReview retrieval returned browser verification. Conference/workshop status was not independently resolved, so the bibliography conservatively labels it an OpenReview manuscript rather than claiming main-conference acceptance. [Primary PDF](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=P7fHU7ZNFQ&name=pdf); [record](https://openreview.net/forum?id=P7fHU7ZNFQ).

## Defensible contribution

This project is a small controlled replication and extension of established behavioral monitoring ideas. It tests a simplified partner-outsider intervention response gap in one binary setting, contrasting a surface convention with an explicit semantic convention. Meaning-changing counterfactuals and a payload-independent semantic placebo probe whether the statistic responds to private convention use rather than information about the designated hidden instruction. The contribution is the controlled diagnostic and its openly logged implementation, not invention of semantic steganography, receiver-outsider comparisons, or behavioral detection.

The focused review did not find this exact combination of a randomized binary convention, semantic counterfactual, and payload-independent placebo in these six papers. This is not proof of priority. The broad syntactic-versus-semantic comparison is substantially duplicated by Anwar et al. Appendix A and the robustness comparison by Zhou and May. State this overlap prominently.

## Interpretation constraints for the report

- A semantic counterfactual intentionally changes meaning; its effect can reflect ordinary semantic sensitivity. It cannot inherit the specificity argument for a meaning-preserving paraphrase.
- If the placebo retains the private mapping while breaking the frame-payload association, a positive response gap with chance payload accuracy is an informative false positive for payload communication. It is not necessarily a false positive for convention use.
- Chance outsider performance in a small sample is not proof of security. Randomized mappings prevent direct task logic from determining the bit, but do not establish indistinguishability against adaptive monitors.
- Scripted senders and templated interventions test a prescribed channel, not autonomous invention, covert intent, emergence, or adaptive evasion.
- Distinguish decoding accuracy gaps from response-change gaps and from the original PoPLi distributional estimator.
